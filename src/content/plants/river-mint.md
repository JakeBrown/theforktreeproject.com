Our October plant of the month is *Mentha australis*, and I’m hoping we’ve come far enough in our language lessons for you to guess what sort of plant it might be and which country it comes from. To confirm: *Mentha* is the mint genus, and *australis*, naturally, refers to Australia. Did you know, though, that the name *Mentha* comes from a Greek myth about a water nymph who was turned into a mint plant?

The common name for this creeping plant is River Mint. This is fitting for a plant that scurries along river and creek edges, sweetening the air around it. Here are 5 reasons it wouldn’t be that bad to turn into a mint plant:

## 1. Traditional uses

River Mint grows across Australia (except for Western Australia – but they have enough to boast about). Being so widespread, it has been used traditionally by many First Nations groups. Kaurna medicinal uses include treatment for colds and headaches (take a look at this resource for more information: [Kaurna Food & Fibre](https://www.aaeesa.org.au/kaurna-food-and-fibre/)). River Mint has also been used more broadly as a digestive tonic and an insect repellent. It is equally at home in the food world – and the flavour packs a punch. It makes a bold meat seasoning and a bright but earthy brewed tea.

## 2. The fresh minty scent

It’s enough to clear your airways! Think of the smell of mint from your home garden and multiply it by 50. Whenever we take groups on a tour of our Rare Seed Orchard here at The Forktree Project, stopping at the *Mentha australis* bed is guaranteed to get a reaction. It’s particularly heart-lifting to see school children pick a leaf, bruise it between their fingers to smell, and widen their smile in surprise. I understand (sort of) that plants aren’t always THE most exciting thing in a person’s day, but when there’s one that smells good enough to roll around in, we can all come together and talk about it.

![River Mint growing around its identification sign in the Rare Seed Orchard](/images/plants/river-mint-bed.jpg)

## 3. Conservation status

Though River Mint is considered common across South Australia generally, it is threatened in some regions. Here on the Fleurieu Peninsula it is listed as Vulnerable and in continuing decline, with grazing and removal of habitat as likely causes. The good news is, like many of our threatened plants, River Mint will thrive if it’s given half a chance. With enough water and space to roam, it can spread its minty wings wide.

## 4. One of many *Mentha*

*Mentha australis* is one of three native mints we currently grow in our nursery, along with *Mentha diemenica* (Slender Mint) and *Mentha satureioides* (Creeping Mint). All three are regionally threatened species. Collectively we grow thousands of these plants each year for restoration across the Fleurieu and now into the mid North. Many of our *Mentha* plants are now at home around the Myponga Reservoir Reserve as part of SA Water’s Reservoirs for Nature project. River Mint can be grown from seed, plugs, and cuttings, depending on timing and need.

## 5. Habitat

*Mentha australis* flowers throughout summer, attracting native bees and butterflies with its tiny whitish blooms. Its groundcover form makes it a safe refuge in damp areas.
