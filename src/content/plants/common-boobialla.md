The August plant of the month is about more than just a plant, really. But then again, it always is — when do they ever grow in isolation?

We’re talking about *Myoporum insulare* or Common Boobialla. *Insulare* means island, which makes sense, because this classic shrub sings of islands and coastal areas. Would you have come across it before? Likely, if you’ve ever been to the beach in the southern half of Australia.

It’s a lush, green, juicy, shiny, salt-tolerant, drought-tolerant, windbreaking, bird-attracting large shrub with purple berries that you should not eat without being sure of what you’re eating and that taste like carrots (taste is debatable). A hero of coastal dune restoration, Common Boobialla prevents erosion with its extensive root network. It’s also inherently fire-resistant. With this many benefits, it has obviously been co-opted for seaside landscape gardening.

![Purple berries growing on a Common Boobialla](/images/plants/common-boobialla-berries.jpg)

This year in the Forktree nursery we grew about 100 Common Boobialla using a mixture of seed and cuttings. Cuttings grow readily, but seed is a challenge — a fun challenge — requiring pre-treatment to stimulate germination. It can still take up to a year! Seedlings emerge as you are about to give up on the whole tray.

Some of this year’s *Myoporum insulare* was local to the Fleurieu and went to surrounding properties. Some was local to Raukkan on the Coorong.

For the past year we’ve partnered with Regenerate Australia and the local Ngarrindjeri community in Raukkan to put 25,000 native plants back into the landscape. This came together in July as a two-part project on Country: large-scale biodiversity restoration and a Community Pocket Forest. Together with the local rangers, we put over 30 species in across both sites. Sixty more Common Boobialla are now spreading their roots in a community meeting place on the bank of Lake Alexandrina.

![Local rangers with Fleur and James during the Raukkan planting project](/images/plants/common-boobialla-raukkan.jpg)

Every plant in the ground is a win, and it feels good to know these are part of a larger story.
